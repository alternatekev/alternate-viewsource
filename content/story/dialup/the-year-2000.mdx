<Glance>
The Wayback Machine's first capture of alternate.org, January 18, 2000, is an empty Apache directory. By June it was a multi-author weblog: PHP3 and MySQL, HTML tables, a footer crediting the design to me and Tai R. Kahn and the code to a friend we'd paid $500. The first post was March 18. On July 7 I wrote a day of my life as an XML-ish language called KCML, with syntax coloring, as an art project.
</Glance>

Every version of this site since is on the timeline, with its stack and its archive capture. This is the first one.

alternate.org started as a group blog for a handful of Denver friends, not a portfolio. Tai and I designed it; a friend wrote the PHP3 and MySQL. The About page promised "the best news, editorial, and commentary on alternate subjects such as: Art, Music, Digital Design, Operating Systems, Extreme (for a lack of a better word) Sports, Apple, Microsoft, RedHat, Technology, Science, Religion, Political Banter, etc..." delivered "in a aggressive but honest manner." We were very young. We reviewed Mac OS X developer previews and complained about Microsoft, and I redesigned the whole thing on a bored Sunday in September. By December readers were voting on the next design, three to one for the blue one, which became v3 in February.

Ten years later I wrote that I hadn't been the best blogger and hadn't written the site alone, but it had always been a place I wouldn't feel right about not having on the web. Twenty-six years later, that's still the reason.

**July 7, 2000.** I'm prone to insomnia. That night I lay in the fugue between wanting sleep and being annoyed at being awake, going back over my day: how long the drive to work took, what I ate, what I did. I pictured a layer under the surface of my life that recorded all of it into a document to be read later, and it struck me that HTML was a pretty good way to capture that. To rest, I had to get up and build it on my 15-inch, gray-translucent computer with a hockey puck for a mouse.

![KCML as it rendered in 2000: a day written as markup, colored like HTML in BBEdit.](/writing/my-days-are-dictated-by-code/screen-shot-2015-08-23-at-9-43-51-am.webp)

I took the main data points of the previous day and codified them into an XML/HTML-type language. A `day` element, 24 hours long, named after me. A `car` with a destination. `task` elements by type and client. A `quake` element with `play="true"`, which was accurate. It was an art project, so it mattered that the code was syntax-colored, so that in a browser it looked like HTML being edited. With a rough draft done the idea was out of my head and I slept. I remember nothing about the next day.

<Inhabit>
KCML is the oldest thing of mine still online, and the one I'd point to if you asked where everything else came from. A lighter version of it won awards in a contest for five-kilobyte web pages the next spring, which is [the next phase's story](#piece-chapter-the5k-and-wall-street). In 2003 another sleepless night turned into a single-user app in PHP; by 2008 that was a personal data project with a thousand users and a calendar drawn as a heatmap. And when I look at KCML now, it's no surprise to me that I love JSX. Markup that describes a life, rendered by a browser. I've been writing the same file for twenty-six years.

What the year taught me: own the domain. Co-owned since 2000, redesigned fifteen times, hosted on everything from a friend's PHP3 to [this](#piece-chapter-this-site). The content moved every time. The address never did.
</Inhabit>
