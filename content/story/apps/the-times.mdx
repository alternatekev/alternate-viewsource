In the fall of 2006, I was part of a small team at Wall Street on Demand that pitched The New York Times on a replacement for their MarketWatch-powered (meaning competitor-powered) financial markets research portal. I'd been with the company a year and was still a relatively new member of the design team, taking much direction from the Creative Director. We continued to meet with the client periodically for about a year before work started in earnest.

The pitch work had been valuable in demonstrating what we could do from a cold start, but we needed to start all over again once things were officially underway. That meant meetings on site with news editors, graphics editors and web designers. We learned the ins and outs of their publishing system (since replaced with WordPress) so we could understand how their content producers' work flowed throughout the day and night. We learned their standards for web design, content design and print-based financial visualization, and wove them together into a new set of rules. We worked with them to forge a hybrid model for displaying financial information on the web that felt at home within their ever-changing site.

Our main work would take over a complete subdomain of their site, so our developers built a new platform on our end that mimicked the look and feel of nytimes.com. I worked hand in hand with the development team to build both HTML and Flash-based tools (hey, it was 2008) for users to investigate market data, and with the client's developers on ways our content could show up inside the news stories themselves. I spent six months designing a 15×10 GIF (among many other things) for the front page of The New York Times.

<Inhabit>
By late 2007, when we started work from scratch, I was running most of the design day to day, and by 2009 I was an Associate Creative Director. I oversaw 60 to 70 percent of the design and front-end engineering work, managing a range of two to three designers at a time. Our Creative Director oversaw the broad scope of the work, but many of the day-to-day management tasks were handled by me.

NYTimes.com Markets launched in 2008, with its Chart Builder.
</Inhabit>
