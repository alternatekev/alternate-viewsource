<Glance>
On September 22, 2026 the Lotus 79 wore version 13 of a shattered-glass livery in the paint viewer. By September 30 there were eighteen cars. On September 24, between 5:56pm and 10:32pm, I built the app that installs them, in six commits.
</Glance>

Every car in the racing service can wear your own paint, if you can produce a 2048-pixel texture that fits a template nobody documents well. For years I drove in the defaults. In September, with the shatter filter from the Doooom Shatter chapter working again, I had a source image I actually wanted on a car.

![The Lotus 79 through ten versions of the livery, v2 to v14.](/writing/racing-rig/2026-09-lotus79-v2-to-v14.jpg)

**The paint.** Matte black, gloss shards, an orange 36. The first car was the Lotus 79, because of course it was. The agent drove Illustrator through scripts: place the shard field on the template, keep the color patches where the template says the body is, obey the rule that the number block stays legible, lay in the logos and the sponsor rows, export the texture and its spec map. Fourteen versions of the Lotus to get the flanks right. Then the same skill, four of them by the end of the day, placed it on seventeen more cars, from a Miata to a Le Mans prototype to the open-wheelers.

**The app.** The service's own paint-sharing tool wants an account and a subscription. I wanted a folder. On September 24 at 5:56pm the first commit says "Build doooom paints: Tauri v2 livery swapper." Clippy clean two minutes later. The library of paints reads straight from its git repository at 6:21. Warning chips at 6:31. The paint shown on its card at 7:34. Backups moved out of the library at 10:32, and done. It backs up what's there, applies the new paint, cleans the cache files the game leaves behind, and refuses to step on the other tool if it's running.

<Inhabit>
Three days later the paints app moved into the one-rig desk from the racing chapter, as its Paints tab. That's where it lives.

Why agents made this possible: a desktop app in Rust for a problem exactly one person has is the definition of software that never gets written. With an agent that already knew Tauri, TGA decoding and the game's folder layout, it's an evening, and the evening is documented to the minute. The Illustrator scripting is the better example, though. I was a designer in the late nineties. I know what I want the car to look like. I had not scripted Illustrator since the plugin era. The agent reads the scripting guide faster than I can find it.

What I learned: the small thing you build for yourself in an evening tells you more about the state of the art than any demo. In 2026 the state of the art is: describe the folder, get the app, drive the car.
</Inhabit>
